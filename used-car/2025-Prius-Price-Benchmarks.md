## The one number to remember

**In Orange County, a used 2025 Prius LE averages an asking price of $25,010. Anything
asking $28k+ for an LE is a bad deal — a brand-new 2026 LE stickers at $29,745.**

The national "average 2025 Prius listing = $30,063" figure you'll see quoted is
misleading. It's inflated by AWD, Nightshade, Limited and Plug-in trims in the mix.
Don't let a salesperson benchmark you against it.

## Price targets by trim

Used 2025, roughly 10k–20k miles, clean history, Orange County. These are **pre-tax
sale prices**, not out-the-door — see the OTD math below.

| Trim (FWD) | 2025 MSRP new | OC average ask | Good price | Great price | Walk away above |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LE | $28,350 | $25,010 | ≤ $24,000 | ≤ $22,500 | $26,500 |
| XLE | $31,795 | $28,130 | ≤ $27,000 | ≤ $25,500 | $29,500 |
| Nightshade | $32,795 | not separately reported | ≤ $28,000 | ≤ $26,500 | $30,000 |
| Limited | $35,365 | not separately reported | ≤ $30,000 | ≤ $28,500 | $31,500 |

AWD adds roughly $1,400 to every trim, new and used. Only pay it if you actually
want it — in Orange County it's close to dead weight.

**Confidence note:** the LE and XLE columns are real market averages. The Nightshade
and Limited rows are my extrapolation from trim spacing and the top of the Edmunds
valuation band ($31,252) — treat those two as directional until we see live listings.

### Sanity anchors
- Edmunds values the whole 2025 Prius range at **$21,697 – $31,252** depending on
  trim, mileage and condition.
- Edmunds clean **trade-in** on a base 2025 with ~12k miles: **$22,577**.
  **Private-party: $24,071.** A dealer buying at $22.5k and asking $28k is taking
  $5,500 of margin off you — that's your negotiating room, and it's real.
- Los Angeles asks run **higher** than Orange County (LE $26,833 vs $25,010).
  Search OC first; only widen to LA if OC inventory is thin.
- Nationally only **29.1%** of 2025 Prius listings rate as a good or great deal.
  Expect to reject two out of three cars you look at. That's normal, not bad luck.

## Out-the-door math (the only number you should ever negotiate)

Garden Grove sales tax is **8.75%**, and it's based on where you *register* the car,
not where you buy it — so a Long Beach or El Monte dealer doesn't change your tax.

```
OTD  =  sale price × 1.0875          (Garden Grove tax)
      + $85                          (CA doc fee — capped by law, see below)
      + ~$450–600                    (DMV title, registration, VLF)
```

**Rule of thumb: OTD ≈ sale price × 1.0875 + $585.**

| Sale price | Estimated OTD |
|---|---|
| $22,500 | ~$25,050 |
| $24,000 | ~$26,685 |
| $25,000 | ~$27,775 |
| $27,000 | ~$29,950 |

### California-specific leverage
- **The doc fee is capped at $85 by law** (CA Vehicle Code §11713.1 — $85 for DMV
  partner dealers, $70 for non-partners). A bill to raise it to $260 was vetoed in
  October 2025. If a dealer's worksheet shows a doc fee above $85, they are breaking
  the cap — say so, in writing, and watch the rest of the numbers get honest fast.
- Everything else on the worksheet that isn't tax, the $85, or DMV fees is
  negotiable: "dealer prep," "market adjustment," VIN etching, nitrogen, paint
  sealant, theft protection. Refuse them line by line.

## Used 2025 vs. new 2026 — worth a look before you commit

| | Used 2025 LE (OC avg) | New 2026 LE |
|---|---|---|
| Price | $25,010 | $29,745 sticker; KBB fair purchase runs $360–$1,090 under |
| Est. OTD | ~$27,775 | ~$32,000 |
| Warranty | ~1 year used up | full, from zero |

**The gap is roughly $4,300 out-the-door.** That justifies buying used — but only at
the *average* ask or better. If the used 2025s you find are sitting at $27k–$28k, the
gap collapses to under $2k and you should just buy the new 2026. Keep this comparison
live during negotiation; it's your strongest single lever.

## Where the Prius sits on depreciation

The Prius is one of the slowest-depreciating cars in its class — 36% over five years,
about 21% over the first three. That cuts both ways:

- **Against you:** used Prius sellers know it and price stubbornly. Discounts come
  slowly and you'll need patience.
- **For you:** whatever you pay, you keep more of it. A one-year-old Prius bought
  near the bottom of the band is genuinely a good asset.

The 2–4 year old window is the actual depreciation sweet spot. A 2025 at one year old
is slightly *before* that sweet spot — which is exactly why the $24k-or-below
discipline on an LE matters. Buy it right or don't buy it.
